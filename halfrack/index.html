How to read the diagrams

  • Gold line is the ball — the handoff, pitch, or the first read on a pass.
  • White lines are everyone else's routes.
  • Numbers on pass plays are the quarterback's reads, in order: look at 1, then 2, then 3.
  • The ⊥ bar means that kid blocks on this play; dashed white is a fake or a lead block in motion. Everyone is eligible — guards can catch, and anyone but the center can run it.
  • Dashed white is a fake or an escort — no ball.
  • Tap ✎ on any play to rename it to whatever your kids call it. The nickname shows everywhere, travels with your team when you share it, and the original stays underneath as "aka".
  • Tap Flip ↔ to run any play to the other side — the whole picture mirrors, players and all. The flip sticks until you flip it back, and travels with your team when you share it.
  • Tap Run it again and hand the phone to the kids — the ball moves so they can watch their job happen.

How many should you actually keep?

A game plan is six to ten plays you can run in your sleep: a dive, a sweep, a keeper, two quick throws, a shot play. Keep those, drill them, and add wrinkles as the season goes.

Two on the line, everyone else covering — the flag pull wins the down. Build your looks here, call them from Game day.

The two on the line

  • Exactly two rushers, never more. A corner walking up to jam a receiver doesn't count.
  • No head-to-head. Shade a shoulder of the guard or back off a full yard.
  • The center is protected until they're up and blocking — don't touch them early.
  • Never strip the ball. Pull flags, knock passes down after they leave the hand, that's it.
  • (3rd/4th grade) Rushers line up in the B gap — inside ear on the guard's outside ear.

Defense rules that decide games

  • A sack is the flag. The passer's flag pulled with the ball still in hand ends the play — no grace for a throwing motion.
  • The spot is the flag pull. The ball goes where it was when the flag came out, or where the carrier left the field.
  • Dive for flags, grab flags not jersey — holding is 10 yards. Never run through the carrier.
  • Coverage contact is legal inside five yards — continuous, inside the frame, ball not in the air.
  • Knock passes down after they leave the hand — never strip the ball.

The short versionWhat decides most games
▼
  • 8 vs 8. Minimum 8 to start; play down to 7 after an injury, never fewer.
  • Everyone is eligible. Four downs to reach the next zone line (the 15, 30, 15). Punt anytime — other team starts on their 5.
  • The QB can run (a sneak needs one step back first). The center can never run the ball.
  • Blocking is legal — open hands, shoulders-to-waist, on your feet. No contact above the shoulders, ever.
  • Defense: exactly 2 on the line. No head-to-head over a lineman — shade a shoulder or back off a yard.
  • Scoring: TD 6 · PAT from the 5 = 1, from the 10 = 2 · Safety 2. Defense can return a PAT pick for 2.
  • Clock: two 24-minute halves, running clock until the last 2 minutes. 30 seconds to snap. Two 30-second timeouts per half.
1
Game basicsField · downs · clock
▼
  • Eight offensive and eight defensive players on the field during live plays; minimum 8 players to start the game. Rosters are 8–16; after an injury a team may play with 7, never fewer.
  • All players are eligible receivers.
  • Four downs to advance to the next zone-line-to-gain (markers at the 15, 30, and 15) for a new set of downs.
  • On any down a team may punt or go for it. A punt gives the other team the ball on their own 5-yard line.
  • The ball is spotted wherever it was at the flag pull or where the carrier left the field of play.
  • Field is 30 × 60 yards with two 10-yard end zones. Each half and every punt starts on the offense's 5.
  • Two 24-minute halves, 2-minute halftime. Running clock except the last 2 minutes of the game, when it stops on incompletions, out-of-bounds, timeouts, and while the ref sets a first down.
  • 30 seconds to snap once the ball is spotted and the coach is in the huddle — one warning, then delay of game. Two 30-second timeouts per half; they don't carry over. Yell "clock check?", not "time?", so you don't accidentally burn one.
  • Injury: clock stops, restarts when the player is off the field. Officials may stop the clock as needed.
2
EquipmentFlags · pads · pre-snap gotchas
▼
  • Required on the field: mouthpiece, soft helmet, soft shoulder pads, league-provided flags, and the league sublimated jersey. Legal flags are the coaches' responsibility.
  • No pockets, belt loops, zippers, or exposed drawstrings (professionally sewn-shut pockets at the official's discretion). Jerseys stay tucked and never cover the flag belt.
  • Flags on the hips, evenly spaced, suction cups down and away, belt snug. No altered, shortened, or knotted flags — that risks ejection or forfeit. Flags can't match your pants color.
  • A towel or anything else worn on the waist counts as part of the flag belt.
  • Cleats are fine, exposed metal never is. No jewelry; no hard braces, casts, or unyielding material. Nails trimmed, taped, or gloved. Soft gloves, elbow/shin/knee pads, taped forearms, eye protection, and knit caps are fine; ball caps come off; hoodies tuck in.
  • Missing or improper flag belt: fix it at the next dead ball or leave the field. A carrier with a bad belt can't advance — they're down where they took possession. Officials will try to call it out before the snap ("number X, down on possession").
3
OffenseLine rules · snaps · runs · passes
▼
  • Minimum four players on the line at the snap, with Guard–Center–Guard no wider than fingertip to fingertip. Players in motion don't count as on the line.
  • One player may be in motion at the snap — parallel to the line only, never toward it.
  • Snap from the ground, fluid and continuous, between the center's legs. Direct snaps to any off-line player are legal.
  • Nobody lines up closer than five yards to the sideline. Entering the neutral zone before the snap is a dead-ball foul.
  • The QB may run at any time. A sneak requires the ball to fully leave the center's hands and one QB step backward before going forward. The center cannot run the ball.
  • Unlimited backward handoffs, laterals, and backward passes. One forward pass per play, thrown from behind the line (any part of the passer's body behind it counts). Once the ball crosses the line it can't come back and be thrown forward legally.
  • The carrier is down when anything but hands or feet touches the ground. The offense retrieves the ball after each play. (3rd/4th grade: inside the 5, bad snaps return to the original line.)
  • Passer's flag pulled with the ball still in hand = sack — no grace for a throwing motion.
  • Intentional grounding: no throwing into the ground, out of bounds, or to empty grass to save yards or clock. A spike must be immediate and fluid after the snap.
  • A catch is one foot in bounds with momentary possession. Simultaneous catch goes to the offense. Ground knocking the ball loose = incomplete. A receiver who stepped out on their own can't be first to touch the pass (illegal touching). No early-pull penalty on a bobbled ball.
  • Ball carriers: jumps, spins, and jump cuts are legal when safe; no hurdling players; no intentional dives or lunges to gain ground; extending the ball for extra yardage is always legal. Avoid defenders who've established position. The defense may dive for flags. Passers may jump to throw.
  • Flag guarding — denying access to your flags in any physical way: stiff arms, swatting hands, pinning with the ball or arm, flailing, extreme shoulder dips. Carry the ball high on the body. Knee dips, cuts, skips, and short hops are legal. Not called when no defender is in range. 10 yards, spot foul, loss of down.
4
BlockingWhat's legal and the long list that isn't
▼
  • Contact between the shoulders and waist only ("inside the frame"), initiated with open hands, blocker on their feet before, during, and after. No contact above the shoulders, ever.
  • Downfield blocking for the carrier is allowed. Blocking downfield while a forward pass is in the air is pass interference (unless the pass is behind the line). Two-on-one is legal if both stay above the waist.
  • Swim moves are legal unless they contact the head, neck, or face.
  • If a blocked player turns their back mid-block, continued contact is legal.
  • Illegal: leading with the shoulder · low/chop/cut blocks · crack-backs · blind-side hits (extended arms and open palms only) · clipping · tripping · hook/hug blocks · rolling blocks · dive blocks · swinging arms or elbows · slapping or punching · grabbing jerseys · interlocked hands · laying on a downed player.
5
DefenseLine limits · rushing · coverage
▼
  • Exactly 2 defenders on the line of scrimmage — no more. A corner walking up to jam a split-out receiver doesn't count against the two.
  • No stripping the ball, ever — including from the QB. No mimicking the offense's signals or cadence.
  • No free plays: once the snapper's hands are on the ball, entering the neutral zone is offside — play blown dead, 5 yards.
  • Rushers can't line up head-to-head over a lineman: shade a shoulder or line up a yard off. The center is a defenseless player until they're up and blocking. (3rd/4th grade: rushers line up in the B gap.)
  • Roughing: avoid charging unprotected players; don't crash the passer's arm or body even if the ball is tipped; you can knock a pass down after it leaves the hand. Contact while blocking a throw risks roughing. Follow-through contact initiated by the passer is not roughing (unless it's to the head/neck/face).
  • Coverage: contact allowed within the first five yards — continuous, inside the frame, ball not in the air. Incidental contact and tangled feet aren't PI. Face guarding without contact is legal.
  • PI examples: push-offs, playing through the back, hook-and-turn, arm bars, grabbing wrists, riding a receiver off their path, pick plays. Catchability doesn't matter — benefit of the doubt goes to the receiver.
  • Defensive PI: 15 yards and an automatic first down (or half the distance), even in the end zone.
  • Interceptions are returnable. An end-zone pick not brought out is a touchback — ball on the 15.
6
Scoring & overtimePoints · the OT shootout
▼
  • Touchdown 6 · PAT from the 5 = 1 point, from the 10 = 2 points (run or pass, coach's choice) · Safety 2. The defense can return a PAT interception for 2.
  • Overtime: coin flip decides who chooses offense or defense first (the choice alternates each round; the flip loser picks which end of the field, and that end stays). Each team gets one play from the 5 (1 point) or the 10 (2 points) — offense's choice. Both teams always get their turn; repeat until decided.
  • All regulation rules and penalties apply. No timeouts in OT.
  • OT interceptions are returnable and worth 2 points — and both teams still get their offensive possession.
7
Game managementCoin toss · challenges · coaches · weather
▼
  • Coin toss: captains bring the game ball for inspection; officials confirm legal uniforms and give the sportsmanship warning. Winner picks offense, defense, a goal to defend, or defers; loser takes what's left; choices reverse for the second half.
  • Challenges: head coach only, procedural errors only (wrong down, wrong yardage) — never judgment calls or no-calls. Call timeout before the next snap; if you're right the timeout isn't charged, if you're wrong with no timeout left it's 15 for unsportsmanlike.
  • Coach on the field: one coach may align players, give the play from a card, and be in the huddle — then get 10 yards behind the line once the huddle breaks. No coaching during the snap (unsportsmanlike).
  • Conduct: profanity-free — players, coaches, and spectators. No disrespectful language, racist or sexist behavior, or bullying; violations can mean dismissal for the year. Fighting is immediate ejection, possibly the season, possibly for life. Officials can silence speakers; teams clean up their garbage.
  • Lightning: detected within 10 miles, everything stops. Shelter safely (not under trees). No play for 30 minutes after the last strike or thunder.
