The rotation that works

With 8 players and 5 on the field, three sit every series. Set the rotation in Game plan before you leave the house — the Auto-rotate button builds the standard one:

  • Series 1: players 1–5 on · 6 7 8 out
  • Series 2: 6 7 8 1 2 on · 3 4 5 out
  • Series 3: 3 4 5 6 7 on · 8 1 2 out
  • Series 4: 8 1 2 3 4 on · 5 6 7 out
  • Then repeat. Everyone sits about the same amount and nobody has to keep track.

Keep center and quarterback to the three kids who practiced them most, and let them move within the group.

Sideline job for the kids who are out

Give the three on the bench a real assignment every series: call the play out loud with you, count the other team's players, or watch one opponent and report back. Bored kids on a sideline is where discipline problems start.

Bring

  • Two footballs, one already inflated properly
  • Spare flag belts — at least two, they break
  • Eight cones
  • Water, plus extra for the kid who forgets
  • Your phone with this page open, and a printed roster as backup

First game, honestly

They will line up wrong, someone will run the wrong way, and at least one kid will take their own flag off. Fix one thing per series, not four. If every kid touched the ball once and wants to come back next week, it was a good game.

Do this before the game: put in your roster, then build each series — who's on the field, who plays where, and who owns each defensive zone. Formations get called on Game day from your kept plays. Check Save & transfer below to make sure your setup sticks on this device.

How to read the diagrams

  • Gold line is the ball — the handoff, pitch, or the first read on a pass.
  • White lines are everyone else's routes.
  • Numbers on pass plays are the quarterback's reads, in order: look at 1, then 2, then 3.
  • The center is eligible — after snapping they release too, usually as the short last-look checkdown (the highest number). On Pancake they stay in to block.
  • Dashed white is a fake or an escort — no ball.
  • Tap ✎ on any play to rename it to whatever your kids call it. The nickname shows everywhere, travels with your team when you share it, and the original stays underneath as "aka".
  • Tap Flip ↔ to run any play to the other side — the whole picture mirrors, players and all. The flip sticks until you flip it back, and travels with your team when you share it.
  • Tap Run it again and hand the phone to the kids — the ball moves so they can watch their job happen.

How many should you actually keep?

Fifty plays is a menu, not a game plan. For a first season with first-graders, four to six total is the real number — two runs, two throws, and maybe one trick play they get excited about. One formation for everything is completely fine and probably better.

Every practice runs the same shape: move, learn one skill, learn one football thing, play a game. Tap a practice to open it. Nothing here needs more than cones, flags, and two footballs.

One each

Man coverage

Your base defense. Each defender picks one offensive player before the snap, says that name out loud, and follows them everywhere.

  • Assign the matchups yourself. Don't let them pick.
  • Fix one thing only: stay closer than arm's length.

Zone boxes

Territory

Three defenders cover short areas, two cover deep. Each owns a box and does not leave it. Use cones in practice so the boxes are real.

  • Use it when one kid on the other team is much faster.
  • Expect this to look messy for two weeks. That's normal.

The chaser

Rush

One defender starts back off the line — usually seven yards, check your league — and runs at the quarterback on the snap with hands up. The other four play ONE EACH.

  • Rotate the chaser every series. Everyone wants this job.
  • Hands up beats speed — deflections are the goal.

Defense rules that decide games

  • Rush from seven yards back. Any number can rush from the rush line. Leave early and cross before a handoff or pass and it's an illegal rush (+5 yards, automatic first down) — step back to the line to reset. Once the ball is handed off, everyone can cross.
  • Nobody has to rush. The 7-second clock forces the throw anyway — cover everyone and let it run.
  • Never touch the passer. Batting the ball down is fine; any contact is roughing the passer (+5, automatic first down).
  • Dive for flags, don't tackle. No holding, no running through the carrier, never strip the ball, and no pulling a flag before the receiver has the ball (+5, automatic first down).
  • The spot is the feet. The ball goes where the carrier's feet were at the pull — not where the ball is.
  • Interceptions are yours at the spot — the only change of possession that doesn't reset to the 5-yard line, and returnable (except on conversions).
  • Don't mimic their signals — copying the offense's cadence is unsportsmanlike conduct.

I
GameCoin toss · possessions · changing sides
▼
  • Captains meet at midfield for the coin toss — the visiting team calls it. The winner chooses offense or defense; the loser chooses direction. The team that started on defense gets the ball to open the second half.
  • The offense takes possession at its own 5-yard line and has three plays to cross midfield. Once across, it has three plays to score a touchdown.
  • If the offense fails to score, or fails to cross midfield, possession changes and the new offense starts its drive on its own 5-yard line.
  • All possession changes except interceptions start on the offense's 5-yard line.
  • Teams change sides after the first half.
II
TerminologyThe words the refs use
▼
  • Line of scrimmage (LOS) — imaginary line through the point of the ball, across the field. Line-to-gain — the line the offense must pass for a first down or score.
  • Rush line — imaginary line seven yards into the defensive side from the LOS.
  • Passer — whoever throws the ball (not necessarily the QB). Rusher — the defender assigned to rush the passer.
  • Downs — three attempts to cross the line-to-gain for a new set or a score.
  • Live ball — while the play is in action; live-ball penalties are part of the play and enforced before the down is complete. Dead ball — immediately before or after a play.
  • Charging — ball-carrier moving illegally straight at a defender with established position (lowering the head, or contact with shoulder, forearm, or chest).
  • Flag guarding — ball-carrier preventing a pull by stiff arm, lowering elbow or head, or blocking access to the flags with hand or arm.
  • Shovel pass — a legal pitch attempted beyond the LOS. Lateral — a backward or sideways toss (illegal in this league — see Running).
III
Eligibility & equipmentWaivers · belts · shoes · jewelry
▼
  • Every player's legal guardian must agree to the online waiver at nflflag.com/form/player before participating.
  • The league provides the official flag belt, NFL FLAG jersey, and footballs.
  • Shoes required. Cleats may not be allowed at some locations; exposed metal cleats are never allowed.
  • Players may tape forearms, hands, and fingers, and may wear gloves, elbow pads, and knee pads. Braces with exposed metal are not allowed.
  • Remove all jewelry, hats, and do-rags. Winter beanies are fine.
  • Jerseys must be tucked in if they hang below the belt line.
  • Shorts or pants without pockets are recommended — pockets must be taped, and games won't be delayed for taping. Flag belts can't be the same color as shorts or pants.
V
Field & rostersNo-run zones · team size · jerseys
▼
  • The field is 30 by 70 yards with two 10-yard end zones and a midfield line-to-gain (some tournaments use smaller fields).
  • No-run zones sit 5 yards before midfield and 5 yards before each end zone. Inside them the ball cannot be run in any fashion — every play must be a pass play, even after a handoff. Each drive meets only two: one to gain the first down, one to score.
  • Stepping on the boundary line is out of bounds.

Rosters

  • Home teams wear dark jerseys, visitors light.
  • Teams are 5 to 10 players and must start with at least five. After an injury with no substitutes left, a team may finish with four on the field — never fewer.
VII
Timing & overtimeClock · timeouts · OT format
▼
  • Two 20-minute halves, continuous clock — it stops only for timeouts and injuries. A 28-point lead ends the game. Halftime is one minute.
  • Once the ball is spotted the offense has 30 seconds to snap — one warning, then delay of game.
  • Each team gets one 30-second timeout per half. Officials can stop the clock at their discretion.
  • On an injury the clock stops, restarting when the injured player is off the field.

Overtime

  • A coin flip decides who's on offense or defense first; the referee picks the end of the field. If a second round is needed, the team that lost the flip chooses, alternating every round after.
  • Teams take turns with one play from the 5-yard line for one point, or the 10-yard line for two. Whether or not the first team converts, the other team gets its chance to win or tie.
  • From the third round on, both teams must go for two from the 10.
  • If the second team fails to beat or match the first team's round, the first team wins. Overtime points are added to the winner's final score; regulation rules and penalties apply, and there are no timeouts.
VIII
Scoring & coachesTD 6 · PAT 1 or 2 · safety 2
▼
  • Touchdown: 6 points.
  • PAT: 1 point from the 5-yard line (pass only) or 2 points from the 10 (run or pass). The scoring team must declare its choice; changing it costs a timeout, and it can't be changed after a penalty. Interceptions on conversions can't be returned.
  • Safety: 2 points — the ball-carrier is down in their own end zone (flag pulled, flag falls out, steps out of bounds, knee or arm down, a fumble in the end zone, or a snap landing in or beyond the end zone).
  • A 28-point lead ends the game. Once the lead is reached no PAT is attempted and the rest of the time is scrimmage mode. Forfeits are scored 28–0.

Coaches

  • Coaches follow NFL FLAG philosophies, coaching guidelines, and the code of conduct.
  • Coaches may be on the field to direct players according to need and division — but must move to the sidelines before the snap.
X
Live ball / dead ballWhen the play is over · no fumbles
▼
  • The ball is live at the snap until an official whistles it dead. Any official can whistle the play dead.
  • Entering the neutral zone is an automatic dead-ball foul — officials may give a "courtesy" warning to move back.
  • A player catching the ball in the air is inbounds if one foot comes down in the field.
  • The defense may not mimic the offense's signals to confuse them — that's unsportsmanlike conduct.
  • Substitutions may be made on any dead ball.

The play is dead when…

  • The ball hits the ground (on a bad snap it's placed where it hit) · the ball-carrier's flag is pulled or falls out · the carrier steps out of bounds or a knee or arm touches the ground · a touchdown, PAT, or safety is scored · a receiver catches the ball with one or no flags · the 7-second pass clock expires · an inadvertent whistle.
  • There are no fumbles. The ball is spotted where the carrier's feet were.
  • On an inadvertent whistle the offense chooses: take the ball where it was (down consumed) or replay the down from the original line of scrimmage.
  • A team may use a timeout to question an official's rule interpretation. If the official was wrong, the ruling is corrected and no timeout is charged; if the ruling stands, the timeout is charged.
XI
RunningNo QB runs · no laterals · no leaving your feet
▼
  • The ball is spotted where the runner's feet are when the flag is pulled — not where the ball is. Forward progress is measured by the front foot.
  • The quarterback cannot directly run with the ball (the QB is whoever receives the snap).
  • Only direct handoffs behind the line of scrimmage — front, behind, or to the side, and multiple handoffs are fine. The "center sneak" is not allowed: the QB can't hand off to the center on the first handoff of the play.
  • Absolutely no laterals of any kind.
  • No running in a live no-run zone (see Field). Anyone who takes a handoff may throw from behind the line. Once the ball is handed off, all defenders are eligible to rush.
  • Runners may not leave their feet to advance the ball — diving, leaping, or jumping to avoid a pull is flag guarding. Spinning is allowed with feet down; spinning out of control is flag guarding. Leaving the feet clearly to avoid a collision is fine.
  • No blocking or screening, ever. Players without the ball must stop moving once the ball crosses the line — no running alongside the carrier.
  • Jerseys tucked in, flags on the hips and unobstructed — deliberately obstructed flags are flag guarding.
XII
Passing & receiving7-second clock · motion · interceptions
▼
  • Passes must be thrown forward from behind the line of scrimmage and received beyond it — a pass caught short of the line is an illegal forward pass. That includes throwaways: the QB may throw it away to avoid a sack, but it must cross the line. Shovel passes are allowed, same rule.
  • The QB has a 7-second pass clock. No throw in time → dead play, down consumed, ball back at the line of scrimmage (same if the QB is standing in the end zone when it expires). A handoff turns the 7-second clock off.
  • Everyone is eligible, including the QB after handing off behind the line.
  • One player may be in motion at the snap — parallel to the line, never toward it.
  • A receiver needs one foot inbounds. Simultaneous catch goes to the offense.
  • Interceptions change possession at the spot of the catch — the only change of possession that doesn't start at the 5-yard line. They're returnable, except on conversions after touchdowns.
XIV
Rushing the passerThe 7-yard rush line
▼
  • Rushers must start at least seven yards off the line of scrimmage (a marker or the referee designates the rush line — have your rushers verify position every play). Any number may rush; players not rushing can defend on the line.
  • Once the ball is handed off the 7-yard rule is off — all defenders may cross.
  • A legal rush: from the 7-yard line, or from anywhere after a handoff. A rusher who leaves early may reset at the rush line, or rush legally if the handoff happens before they cross the line.
  • Penalties: rusher leaving early and crossing before a handoff or pass — illegal rush; any defender crossing before the snap — offside; a defender not on the rush line crossing before a pass or handoff — illegal rush (all +5 yards and automatic first down).
  • Teams don't have to rush (the 7-second clock still runs) and don't have to identify the rusher before the play.
  • Rushers may try to block the pass, but no contact with the quarterback whatsoever — contact is roughing the passer.
  • The offense can't impede the rusher — they have the right to a clear path. A moving offensive player in the path is an impeding-the-rusher penalty; if the offensive player is set, the rusher must go around.
  • A sack is the QB's flags pulled behind the line; ball placed at the QB's feet. A sack in the offense's own end zone is a safety.
XV
Flag pullingLegal pulls · flag guarding
▼
  • A legal pull happens when the ball-carrier is in full possession. Defenders may dive to pull flags but can't tackle, hold, or run through the carrier — and it's illegal to strip or pull the ball.
  • If a flag falls off during the play, the player is down immediately upon possession; the ball is placed where the flag lands.
  • Don't intentionally pull flags off a player who doesn't have the ball.
  • Flag guarding — stiff-arming, dropping the head, hand, arm, or shoulder, or covering the flags with the jersey — is a penalty.
XVI
Formations & conductLine rules · snaps · sportsmanship
▼
  • The offense needs at least one player on the line of scrimmage (the center) and may have up to four. The QB must be off the line.
  • One player at a time may go in motion, 1 yard behind and parallel to the line — never toward it. A set player moving, or motion toward the line, is a false start.
  • The center must snap the ball between the legs in one rapid, continuous motion, and it must completely leave their hands.

Unsportsmanlike conduct

  • Intentional tackling, elbowing, cheap shots, blocking, or any unsportsmanlike act: the game stops and the player is ejected — referee's discretion, no appeals. Foul play is not tolerated.
  • Offensive or confrontational language: one warning, then ejection. No physical or verbal abuse of any opponent, coach, or official.
  • Ball-carriers must make an effort to avoid defenders with established position; defenders may not run through the ball-carrier when pulling flags.
  • Fans: cheer your players (don't harass officials or the other team), keep it clean, compliment all players. Keep kids and gear 10 yards off the field in the end-zone area, stay out from between fields, and use the trash cans.
  • Penalty: defense +10 yards and automatic first down; offense −10 yards and loss of down.
XVIII
PenaltiesWho calls what · the full table
▼
  • The referee calls all penalties and judges incidental contact. All penalties are assessed from the line of scrimmage except spot fouls.
  • Only the team captain or head coach may ask about rule clarifications — players may not question calls.
  • Games can't end on a declined defensive penalty. Live-ball penalties are assessed before the play is considered complete. Yardage more than half the distance to the goal becomes half the distance.

Defensive spot fouls

  • Pass interference — automatic first down
  • Holding — automatic first down
  • Stripping — +10 yards and automatic first down

Offensive spot fouls

  • Screening, blocking, or running with the ball — −10 yards and loss of down
  • Charging — −10 yards and loss of down
  • Flag guarding — −10 yards and loss of down

Defensive penalties

  • Unnecessary roughness · unsportsmanlike conduct — +10 yards and automatic first down
  • Offside · illegal rush (starting inside the 7-yard marker) · illegal flag pull (before the receiver has the ball) · roughing the passer · taunting — +5 yards from the line of scrimmage and automatic first down

Offensive penalties

  • Unnecessary roughness · unsportsmanlike conduct — −10 yards and loss of down
  • Offside / false start · illegal forward pass · pass interference · illegal motion (more than one player moving) · delay of game · impeding the rusher · illegal procedure — −5 yards from the line of scrimmage and loss of down
